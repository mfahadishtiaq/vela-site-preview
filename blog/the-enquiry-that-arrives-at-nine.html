The enquiry that arrives at nine

Published 11 February 20262 min read

Written byFahad IshtiaqFounder, Vela

It is nine in the evening. A message comes in through your website: is there anything open on Thursday morning?

You can answer from the couch. Or leave it until tomorrow and hope they wait.

An AI front desk offers a third option: give the enquiry a useful next step while you get on with your evening.

Help the person move forward

For a booking enquiry, the workflow needs access to your calendar and clear rules about what it can offer. It can check availability, suggest suitable times and record the customer’s choice.

Confirm the appointment only after the calendar accepts it. If the connection fails or the request needs your approval, tell the customer the booking is pending and pass it to you.

The exchange should leave them knowing where they stand. A confirmed time is useful. So is a clear message that you will follow up. An uncertain booking presented as settled creates another problem for the morning.

Make it sound like you

Your greetings. Your phrasing. The details you always mention before someone books.

Build the replies around those habits and your approved service information. Read examples before they go live. Would you send that message? Does it answer the question? Is the next step clear?

Keep changing details, such as availability and pricing, tied to the records you use to run the business. Give unusual questions a clear route to you. Review messages as the service changes, so the replies keep up.

Keep the difficult conversations with you

A complaint needs attention. A complicated quotation needs judgement. Someone asking for an exception may need a conversation.

Decide which requests come to you and how you will receive them. Include what the customer asked, what has already been said and what still needs an answer. They should not have to tell the whole story twice.

Know what happened overnight

In the morning, a short summary should show you what was booked, what is still pending and who needs a reply. A failed calendar action belongs there too. You need to see the loose ends before they become surprises.

Review accuracy and correction time alongside response speed. The useful measure is whether the enquiry moved forward with less work for you.

The enquiry still arrives at nine. Give it a clear route to the next step, and you can put your phone down knowing where to pick things up in the morning.
